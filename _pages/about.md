I am an incoming first-year Ph.D. student in [Management Science and Operations Management](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/phd/dissertation-areas/management-science-and-operations-management) at the University of Chicago Booth School of Business, in the [Stevens Doctoral Program](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/phd). Before Booth, I studied at Stanford University, where I completed a BS in Mathematics and an MS in Computer Science.

I work in theoretical computer science, at the intersection of algorithms, combinatorics, and optimization. Broadly, I am interested in applying discrete mathematics, probability, and complexity theory to problems in this space. My more specific interests are in approximation algorithms and hardness of approximation for **stochastic matching** and **welfare maximization** problems, together with their applications to resource allocation in online platforms and markets.

Most recently, my undergraduate honors thesis with Jan Vondrák introduced *stochastic matching with batch allocations*, a variant of online stochastic matching in which agents receive batches of items. I proved hardness-of-approximation results via new reductions, and gave randomized approximation algorithms that beat the classical $1 - 1/e$ ratio by combining a global maximum matching with an LP relaxation.

Research interests
======
* Approximation algorithms and hardness of approximation
* Matching theory, stochastic and online matching
* Submodular optimization and welfare maximization
* Algorithmic game theory and resource allocation in markets
* Combinatorics and enumeration (meanders, self-avoiding walks, heapable permutations)
