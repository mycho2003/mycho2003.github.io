Research experience
======
* **Stochastic Matching with Batch Allocations**, 2024&ndash;present
  * Stanford University, advised by Jan Vondrák
  * Defined a variation on stochastic matching problems in which agents receive "batches" of items. Proved hardness-of-approximation results using novel constructions, and gave approximation algorithms beating the classical 1 &minus; 1/e approximation ratio. Honors thesis.

* **Approximation Algorithms for Online Stochastic Matching**, 2023&ndash;2024
  * Stanford University, advised by Professor Amin Saberi and Tristan Pollner
  * Studied existing and novel algorithms for the online bipartite matching problem under polynomial-time constraints. Developed computational bounds and rigorous proofs of performance against the optimal online benchmark.

* **Optimal Constructions for DNA Self-Assembly of k-Regular Graphs**, 2023
  * Summer@ICERM, Institute for Computational and Experimental Research in Mathematics
  * Created novel bounds and constructions within a graph-theoretic model of nanostructure self-assembly. Used computational tools, combinatorics, and algebra to analyze optimal configurations and prove useful properties. Accepted for publication in *Involve*.

* **Counting the Number of Meanders**, 2023
  * Stanford University, directed reading with Jacopo Borga
  * Studied the best known asymptotic bounds and methods of computing the number of meanders. Examined and disproved a related conjecture using computational methods.

* **Efficient Methods of Calculating the Number of Heapable Permutations**, 2020&ndash;2021
  * PROMYS, Boston University
  * Discovered and proved novel results in a variation on the Longest Increasing Subsequence problem based on heaps. Improved the time complexity of the best-known algorithm for counting heapable permutations. Published in *Discrete Applied Mathematics*.

* **Given Certain Conditions, Even Selfish Prisoners Can Learn to Cooperate**, 2019&ndash;2020
  * Studied optimal strategies for the Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma through computer simulation, using the n-player version of the game and computational game theory to identify conditions under which cooperation naturally arises. Published in *ActonScholars*.

* **Game of Life to Simulate Computers**, 2019&ndash;2020
  * Investigated methods of constructing logic gates and memory within Conway's Game of Life, using glider mechanics to simulate basic logic. Published in *iJournals*.
