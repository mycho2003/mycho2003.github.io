We study efficient DNA self-assembly within a graph-theoretic model, asking how to minimize the resources needed to construct a target structure.

Our group identified a graph property we call *unswappability*, which leads to optimal constructions with a vertex-cover structure; I developed an algorithm that fully solves the problem in the unswappable case. I also observed that for general $k$-regular graphs, a similar vertex-cover construction can be employed under a small set of restrictions. These methods significantly improve upper bounds for several graph families of interest, and resolve a number of open problems concerning families such as the Rook's graphs.

This work was carried out at the Summer@ICERM REU at the Institute for Computational and Experimental Research in Mathematics, and was presented at the Joint Mathematics Meetings in 2024.
